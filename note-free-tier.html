Why we catalogue the free tier

A free tier is only useful if you know where it stops.

16 November 2026Baseline

Most lists of free services tell you what you get and stop there. The number that decides whether you can actually ship — requests per day, seats, storage, build minutes, how long an idle project stays warm — is the one that gets left out.

So the reference page quotes it. Every entry names the service, what it provides, and the limit as the vendor stated it on 16 November 2026. Ninety-five services across sixteen categories, chosen for the work this collective actually does: build, ship and operate. The source is free-for.dev, which is broader than our slice and worth reading on its own.

Two things follow from that. First, the page is dated on purpose. A free tier is a promise that changes quietly, and the pricing page always wins over any catalogue, including ours. Second, it is a page engineers keep rather than read once — which is why it works with JavaScript off, filters down to the service you are looking for, and lets you star the handful you actually use and copy that list out.

The point is not to collect links. It is to take the cost question out of the first project. The gate is rarely the talent; it is the bill that arrives before the first user.
